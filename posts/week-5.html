Week 5 · Build the corpus yourself

Two-thirds of the vocabulary arrives with the first fifty pages — now build it your way

This week the playground handed us the other half of the Marvel crawl: the text of every hero's Wikipedia page. We treat the corpus as a vocab machine and turn its growth into a game: add characters one at a time — by Zipf deviation, corpus one-offs, live Heaps gain or at random — and watch your corpus's merged word-frequency curve race the idealized Zipf curve (slope −1). One explorable, log–log or linear axes, your call.

A corpus instead of a graph

↑

The week 1 network told us who mentions whom; the week 5 release is the actual prose — the full plain text of the same 303 Wikipedia articles (4.5 MB frozen on 2026-08-26). Tokenizing everything — lowercase, keep apostrophes, strip punctuation — gives 727,440 tokens containing 26,952 distinct words. The median page is only 1,238 tokens, but the corpus is front-heavy: Spider-Man's article alone is 10,293 tokens, over 8× the median.

Everything numeric below was computed once, offline, in analysis/build_week5.py (stdlib only) and frozen into data/marvel_pages/week5_summary.json — the browser only looks numbers up and draws them.

The duel: build a corpus by Zipf's rule, corpus one-offs, Heaps' gain or chance

↑

The metrics of this week answer different questions, so we made them race — via one dropdown, not a wall of buttons. It drives both the manual + One step button and auto-play at once: pick Zipf deviation (the precomputed page whose rank–frequency slope bends hardest away from the ideal −1), corpus one-offs (the remaining page holding the biggest share of its tokens in words used exactly once in the corpus), Heaps gain (defined live, the way Heaps' score must be: the number of brand-new words a page would deposit right now, which depends on everything already added), or a random pick. + One step adds one page by the dropdown's choice, ▶ Play keeps adding at ~10 a second until the corpus is full (switch the dropdown live mid-run); or type any character's name and add it by hand.

Each addition draws that character's own Zipf curve on the chart (log–log by default, linear on demand), behind the merged vocabulary curve of everything you've built so far, with the idealized Zipf curve (slope −1) as the dashed reference. The layers are checkbox toggles; the left panel summarizes the page you just added, including its live marginal gain ΔV — the words it deposits on top of everything you already own.

Watch for the reversal: buying Zipf outliers (spiky minor stubs) one after another keeps your merged curve far below the ideal line — you accumulate vocabulary that is rare in the aggregate but strangely concentrated page by page. The Heaps setting, by contrast, buys words that show up nowhere else in what you already own. Which strategy pulls the merged curve closest to the ideal is the duel; press Play and see.

What we take away

↑

This week the Marvel data got its words back, and the corpus behaved exactly like a corpus: Heaps' β ≈ 0.51, and two-thirds of the vocabulary banked by the 50 most-linked pages — Spider-Man's article, first in the linked-first telling, arrives with 10,293 brand-new words. From there every extra page adds less and less: the duel's live Heaps setting never stops finding words, but each press buys a smaller and smaller slice. The hubs are where vocabulary accumulates; the margins are where it gets minted — one word at a time.

The number that surprised us most: two-thirds of the corpus's words live in just the first 50 of 303 pages (45% of the tokens), and almost no reachable reading order of whom you read first changes the ending — whoever reads the whole thing converges on the same 26,952 words. The build is about the journey, which is exactly why it's an explorable.
