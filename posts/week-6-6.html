The analogy slot machine

The week's most famous demonstration is word arithmetic: king − man + woman ≈ queen. Does the same trick work one level up — start from a character, subtract a word, add a word, and let the machine name the closest superhero? Pull the lever:

How a character becomes a vector

↑

Same recipe as the Marvel map: a character's position is the TF-IDF-weighted average of their fingerprint words (names removed) in our Marvel-trained Word2Vec space. Trade a word and the traded vectors have to be vocabulary the machine actually knows — 3,791 shipped words, everything else gets bounced. The new direction is scored against all 303 character positions, and the podium is who's closest. If you already know the hero you expected, pick them too — the machine reports their exact rank, cosine, and who got in the way. Nothing is precomputed; every spin is live arithmetic.

What the machine gets right (and wrong)

↑
  • The biology round works. The default preset, Spider − man + woman, puts Spider-Girl right at the top of the family (with Wild Child, the machine's own punchline, at #1) — and the moved-words row shows why: man-carrying fingerprints pushed out, woman-adjacent ones pulled in.
  • Some rounds land in the right family. Luke Cage − street + magic crowns Doctor Druid — not the Doctor you ordered, but a real sorcerer from the same neighborhood of the space, with Doctor Strange himself close behind. The analogies rarely name the exact hero; they reliably name the right kind of hero.
  • And some rounds are honest failures. The echo trap: Venom − aliens + technology returns Eddie Brock — the direction barely escapes the symbiote and re-lands on its host, because Venom's fingerprint is about being a symbiote wearing a man. Storm − weather + mind drifts to Satana, a mystic, instead of a telepath. The evidence drawer under each spin is there exactly for rounds like these.

Limitation

↑

A character is reduced to a dozen weighted words, the space to 100 dimensions learned from 727k tokens, and every round inherits the corpus's biases — the war↔home lean that the map post caught. Treat the machine as a comedy writer with a statistics degree: sometimes strangely right, always worth auditing.
