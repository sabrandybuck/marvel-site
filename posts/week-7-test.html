Links vs. meaning

The Marvel supervillain network next to the same 370 characters wired by all-MiniLM-L6-v2 document similarity instead of Wikipedia links.

What we asked

Week 1 built the Marvel network out of what pages point at: a directed edge exists when one article's wiki-source links to another. Week 7 of the course turns pages into meaning: a pretrained encoder maps every page to a vector, and two pages belong together when those vectors are close. Those are two very different notions of "near", and they do not have to draw the same picture.

So on this test page we asked the simplest comparative question we could think of: take the Marvel Comics supervillains dataset — 370 characters crawled from Wikipedia's supervillain category — and draw it twice. Once as the hyperlink network it actually is. Once as a "skeleton": the same 370 nodes, but with each character connected to its nearest neighbours in embedding space, as if text similarity had replaced Wikipedia's linking decisions. What survives both drawings — and what only makes sense in one of them — is the whole story.

What we did

We fetched the plain text of all 370 villain pages from Wikipedia's extracts API (522,110 words total, median page 795 words — more than the encoder's whole window). Each page was embedded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384 dimensions). The encoder cuts input at max_seq_length = 256 word pieces, which would silently throw away most of every page — the course's week 7 page warns about exactly this truncation trap. Following the corpus atlas, we cut each page into passages of at most 180 words along sentence boundaries, embedded every passage, and took the normalized mean of the passage vectors (3,281 passages across the corpus). "Nearest neighbours" below is always cosine similarity in that original 384-d space, never the 2D drawings. Node colors in both graphs below are Louvain communities of the link network, so you can watch one grouping fall out of the other representation.

What surprised us

The two pictures disagree, and not at the margins. Take every villain's five nearest pages by cosine similarity in the original 384-dimensional space: that produces 1,519 semantic edges, of which only 218 (14.4%) also exist as a Wikipedia link in either direction. Flip it around and the link network is even more different: 604 of the 910 link pairs (66%) never show up in either node's top-10 semantic neighbours. Only 195 of 370 villains (52.7%) have even one top-5 meaning neighbour that is also a link neighbour. The half that do — and the green edges in explorer 2 — are mostly the hub-and-spin-off families: Spider-family, Hulk-family, X-adjacent names.

What the encoder finds that the links never wired is the fun part. It reconstructs whole corners of the universe from nothing but prose: the Spider-cosmos (Scarlet Spider ↔ Spider-Woman 0.81, Scarlet Spider ↔ Spider-Girl 0.80, Blood Spider ↔ Scarlet Spider 0.74 — none of these pairs link to each other), and the name-twins the linker never had reason to connect (Sentry (Kree) ↔ Sentry (Robert Reynolds) 0.76, the zodiac pairs Leo ↔ Libra 0.76, Aries ↔ Gemini 0.73). Some "meaning-only" pairs are genuinely about-ness rather than story connection: Leader ↔ Maestro (0.79) are both Hulk-derived archetypes, and the tiny stub pages Naga ↔ Tundra (0.80) are the same kind of article even though they have nothing to do with each other.

What the links find that the encoder does not is the flip side: most hyperlink edges exist for reasons the prose statistics barely register — "this villain's current story arc fights the Hulk this month", event-crossover cross-references, navigate-here editorial choices. An edge built from links measures who a page discusses; an edge built from MiniLM measures what a page is like. Neither is the network of the supervillains; they are two different measurement instruments on the same 370 objects, and the 14% where they overlap is about the only part you'd want to call structure in both senses.

A modeling footnote we had to live: with max_seq_length = 256 word pieces and a median page of 795 words, plain truncation would have discarded roughly three quarters of every article. The 180-word passage rule above is the modeling choice that keeps the whole page in the picture — change it and these numbers move (the course's own corpus atlas got different but similar results with the same rule on the heroes).
