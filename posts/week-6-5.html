Week 6 · Go-nuts question

Which characters live between topics?

One of the 6.11 openers asks: find the pages with unusually mixed LDA topic proportions — do those characters really bridge themes, or is the model confused? We asked, we drew one figure, and we dragged the actual sentences in underneath as evidence.

The ask The figure What it said Limitation

The ask

↑

Representation: the week-6 names-removed LDA fit (8 topics, seed 0) — in that fit every page is a mixture of themes: mutant life, symbiotes & hosts, media boilerplate, and friends. A page that is 98% one theme is an easy label. A page at 47%/28% is the interesting one. We measured each page's mixture entropy (how evenly its topic shares are spread) and sorted all 303 pages by it.

What it said

↑
  • Almost half the corpus refuses a single label. 146 of the 303 pages put at least a fifth of their mass on a second topic. The purest pages are the satisfying ones — Eddie Brock, Morbius, Spider-Man, Venom, Black Cat — franchises the fit recognises instantly.
  • The straddlers sit at the borders of the graph, too. Of the 20 most ambiguous pages, 14 have at least 5 of their top-10 TF-IDF neighbors outside their own Louvain community. Ricochet, the Crippler, the Caretaker and friends are textually ambiguous and structurally on the fence — two independent lenses agreeing on who belongs nowhere cleanly.
  • But the model has a bias worth knowing. In 12 of those top 20, one of the two top topics is media boilerplate — the voiced, playable, film, list mixture that popular pages end with. Some crossings are real (Ghost Rider — screen-and-sword mysticism, check the drawer); some are just Wikipedia's infoboxes in topic clothing.

Limitation

↑

One frozen fit. Change the seed and the top-20 straddler list reshuffles; the boilerplate topic's grip on popular pages is the fit's signature, not the corpus's law. The ranking tells you where to look — the drawer's sentences are as far as it can take you, and the verdict stays human.
