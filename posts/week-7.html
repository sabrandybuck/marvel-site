Week 7 · From meaning to measurement

Semantically close, structurally far

The week's question: who is semantically close but structurally far away? We have two lenses over the same 370 Marvel supervillains — a language model that reads each Wikipedia page and turns it into a vector, and the hyperlink network that connects those pages. The pairs where the two lenses disagree are the interesting ones: characters whose pages talk about similar things but never link to each other.

Two lenses, one universe

↑

Every supervillain page is measured twice. The semantic lens is all-MiniLM-L6-v2, a pretrained sentence encoder: each page is cut into passages of at most 180 words, every passage is embedded into a 384-dimensional vector, and the page's vector is the mean of its passage vectors. Two pages are semantically close when their vectors have high cosine similarity — they talk about similar things, even if they don't share vocabulary.

The structural lens is the Wikipedia hyperlink network from Week 2: 370 nodes, 1,136 directed edges. An edge A → B means page A links to page B. Two characters are structurally close when the shortest path between them in this network is short — ideally 1 (directly linked), but 2 or 3 is still nearby.

The divergence score combines the two: divergence = cosine / (path_length + 1). It is high when the pages are semantically similar (high cosine) but structurally far (long path, or no path at all — disconnected pairs get path = ∞). These are the pairs the explorer highlights in red.

Why would two pages be close in meaning but far in structure? Wikipedia's linking conventions are editorial, not semantic. A page about a minor 1970s villain might never link to the page of a thematically similar character from a different era, team, or franchise corner — even though a language model reads both and sees the same themes. The gap between the two lenses is a map of what Wikipedia editors chose not to connect.

How to read it

↑
  • The map is a projection. PCA squeezes 384 dimensions into 2 (explaining about 8% of the variance), so visible distance is only a rough guide. The cosine and path-length numbers in the detail panel are computed in the full spaces — trust those over what your eyes say.
  • Blue = semantically close. A blue line means the two pages have cosine similarity above your threshold — the language model reads them as talking about similar things.
  • Red = divergent (on top of blue). A red line means the pair is also structurally far: separated by at least your path-length threshold in the hyperlink network, or in different connected components entirely. Every red line sits on top of a blue line — a divergent pair is always semantically close.
  • Sliders control everything. The cosine slider sets how "close" means close. The path slider sets how "far" means far. The max-lines slider caps how many red lines appear (the most divergent pairs are shown first). What you set is what you see — no hidden pre-computed lists.
  • Click a dot to see that villain's semantic neighbors (from the MiniLM kNN), their Wikipedia links, and their personal "divergent pairs" list — the characters closest in meaning but farthest in structure.
  • Click a red line to read the opening paragraphs of both pages side by side. The question to ask: why does the model think these are similar, and why doesn't Wikipedia link them?

What we found

↑

Of the 3,550 unique pairs in the semantic neighborhood graph, 598 are disconnected in the hyperlink network — no path at all, not even through intermediaries. The median connected pair sits at a path length of 3. The 50 most divergent pairs (highest cosine among those with path ≥ 3 or disconnected) reveal three recurring patterns:

Same name, different characters

Sentry (Curtis Elkins) ↔ Sentry (Robert Reynolds)
cos 0.726 · path 3 · divergence 0.181

The two Sentinels are unrelated characters who happen to share a code-name. Their pages are semantically close because both are about a super-powered "Sentry" — the model sees the shared name and the shared power-set framing. But Wikipedia's disambiguation keeps them apart: Curtis Elkins' page links into the Vault/Thunderbolts corner, while Robert Reynolds' page links into the Avengers/cosmic corner. Two Sentinels, two neighborhoods, no bridge.

"Doom" by any other name

Doom 2099 ↔ Doomsday Man
cos 0.690 · disconnected

Doom 2099 is a futuristic anti-hero; Doomsday Man is a 1970s Captain Marvel villain — a robot-scientist fusion. Both carry "Doom" in the name and both are about world-ending power, so the embedding places them close. But they exist in entirely different corners of the Marvel universe (Marvel 2099 vs. the Silver Surfer/Ms. Marvel legacy) and the hyperlink network has no path between them at all.

The Spider-verse without the spider-links

Blood Spider ↔ Spider-Woman
cos 0.692 · path 3
Kingpin (Matt Murdock) ↔ Spider-Woman
cos 0.686 · path 3

Spider-Woman appears in multiple divergent pairs. Her page is a disambiguation hub — "Spider-Woman is the code name of several fictional characters" — so the embedding is pulled toward the Spider-Man family's themes (clones, symbiotes, alternate costumes). Blood Spider and Kingpin (Matt Murdock) are thematically adjacent but structurally distant: they link into the Spider-Man pages, not to Spider-Woman's disambiguation page directly.

The Shepard cluster

Naga ↔ Shepard: cos 0.688 · disconnected
Mister Sinister ↔ Shepard: cos 0.665 · disconnected
Emma Frost ↔ Shepard: cos 0.651 · disconnected

Shepard (comics) is a minor X-Men villain whose page is semantically close to several major mutants — Naga, Mister Sinister, Emma Frost — because all are X-Men-adjacent mutants with similar power descriptions. But Shepard's page sits in a small disconnected component of the hyperlink network, so the structural distance is infinite even though the semantic distance is small.

Batroc ↔ Batwing: the "Bat" trap

Batroc the Leaper ↔ Batwing (Marvel Comics)
cos 0.689 · path 3 · divergence 0.172

A pure surface-similarity case: both names start with "Bat". Batroc is a Captain America villain (a French mercenary who can leap); Batwing is a minor Batman-analog in the Marvel universe. The embedding picks up the name overlap and the shared "vigilante-adjacent fighter" framing. Wikipedia's link structure keeps them in different teams' corners.

The Leader's gamma neighbors

Leader ↔ Radioactive Man: cos 0.704 · path 3
Leader ↔ Tundra: cos 0.674 · path 3

The Leader (a Hulk villain) is semantically close to other gamma-powered characters — Radioactive Man, Tundra — because their pages share the vocabulary of radiation, gamma mutation, and Hulk rogues' galleries. The link network routes through Hulk's page rather than connecting the villains directly, creating a path of 3.

The pattern across all of these: the embedding groups by theme, the link network groups by editorial adjacency. A language model sees "gamma-powered villain" and places the Leader next to Radioactive Man. Wikipedia's editors see "this page is about Hulk's rogues' gallery" and link both to Hulk — but not to each other. The divergent pairs are where these two grouping logics part ways.

Limitations

↑

The MiniLM encoder reads at most 256 word pieces per passage; with our 180-word chunking rule, long pages are summarized as the mean of several passage vectors. This is a modeling choice — a single vector for a 5,000-word biography necessarily loses detail. The cosine similarities are computed in this compressed space, not on the raw text.

The hyperlink network is a snapshot of Wikipedia's Marvel Comics supervillains category as crawled in September 2026. Wikipedia's linking is editorial: pages link to what their authors chose to link to, which reflects narrative importance, recency, and editor conventions more than semantic similarity. A path length of 3 does not mean "unrelated" — it means "not directly connected, and not connected through one intermediary."

Disconnected pairs (path = ∞) are mostly small, isolated pages in non-giant components of the link graph — 43 isolates and several small clusters. Their semantic neighbors are often major villains whose pages are in the giant component. The divergence score treats them as maximally far, which is defensible but blunt: a 2-node component and a 50-node component are both "disconnected" from the giant.
