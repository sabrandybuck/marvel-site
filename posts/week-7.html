Who is semantically close but structurally far away?

We embed the Wikipedia text of 370 Marvel supervillains, find pairs of pages that read almost alike, and then check how many links it takes to get from one to the other in the collected link network. Five pairs, each checked against the actual page text.

Two kinds of “close”

↑

A Wikipedia page can be close to another page in two different ways. It can be close in text: the two articles talk about similar things, in similar words. Or it can be close in the link network: one article links to the other, or both link to the same neighbours, so a reader can click from one to the other in a few steps.

These two notions usually agree, but not always. Links are chosen by human editors, one article at a time, and an editor only links what they happen to think of. Text embeddings, on the other hand, only see the words. This week we look for the disagreements: pairs of supervillains that read as near-twins but that the link network keeps many links apart.

Data and method

↑

Pages. We use the 370 pages of Wikipedia’s Category:Marvel Comics supervillains (crawled 2026-09-13 with crawler/). The plain text of each page was fetched from Wikipedia’s extracts API by analysis/fetch_week7_pages.py: 370 of 370 pages, about 522,000 words in total, median page 795 words.

Semantic similarity

Everything here is done by analysis/build_week7_test.py, and this is what the code does:

  1. Each page’s text is cut into passages of at most 180 words, breaking only at sentence ends (a very long single sentence stays whole). The 370 pages give 3,281 passages.
  2. Every passage is embedded separately with the frozen all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence model, with the vectors normalised to length 1. The model reads at most 256 word-pieces (max_seq_length is 256 in the stored metadata), so a passage that is longer than that is truncated.
  3. The page vector is the plain average of the page’s passage vectors, divided by its own length so that it again has length 1.
  4. The similarity of two pages is the cosine similarity of their page vectors, which for unit vectors is just their dot product.

analysis/build_week7_pairs.py runs exactly this procedure for all 370 pages and takes the dot product of every pair of page vectors, giving 68,265 cosines (one per unordered pair, in data/week7/week7_pairs.tsv). The earlier file data/week7/week7_supervillains.json kept only each page’s 12 nearest neighbours; the new cosines agree with those stored scores to within their three-decimal rounding (4,440 scores compared, largest difference 0.0005). The cosines below are the four-decimal values from the pair table.

Structural distance

The link network comes from the same crawl. It is a directed graph with 370 nodes and 1,136 edges: an edge A → B means the article of A links to the article of B. Only links between two of these 370 category pages are in the data. A “path” therefore means a chain of links that stays inside this collected graph; it says nothing about how close two pages are in the whole of Wikipedia, where a link to a hero, a god or a team page could shorten the route a lot. The “distance” in this report is the number of links followed on the shortest directed path from A to B, and we give it for both directions because the graph is not symmetric.

We only feature pairs where a directed path exists in the collected graph (in at least one direction). Of the 68,265 pairs of pages, 18,561 have a directed path in both directions and 24,781 in one direction only (13,324 from the page whose identifier sorts first to the other, 11,457 the other way round); the remaining 24,923 have no directed path in either direction and are left out of the featured examples entirely. The graph has 46 weakly connected components: one giant component with 323 villains, two pairs and 43 villains with no link in or out.

Choosing the pairs

From all 68,265 pairs we kept those with a cosine of at least 0.60 and a shortest directed path of at least 5 links in at least one direction, and left out three very long pages (“Naga”, “Tundra” and “Shepard”, 6,500 to 18,000 words) that sit near many other pages (they account for 79 of the 128 pairs that otherwise pass). That leaves 49 pairs: 16 with a path in both directions and 33 in one direction only. The five pairs below were chosen from the seven with a cosine of at least 0.66, after reading the pages. Masked Marauder–Mysterio (0.678, 5 links each way) is moved to the extra candidates because Mysterio’s page is long (7,543 words) and we only read its opening, and Power Broker–Professor Power (0.666, 6 links) is left out because after reading both pages the main thing we could see them sharing is the word “Power”. So the selection is partly by hand and the order below is not a ranking.

1. Firebrand and Firepower: armour-era Iron Man villains

Firebrand and Firepower are both alias pages shared by several characters (“the name of several supervillains” and “an alias used by two supervillains”), and the first character of each fights Iron Man. Firebrand’s Gary Gilbert is an enforcer for the industrialist Justin Hammer who first appears in Iron Man #27; Firepower’s Jack Taggert wears an experimental armour built to stop Iron Man during the “Armor Wars”, and the second Firepower’s armour is disabled by a War Machine EMP blast. Both pages are also about armoured or technology-backed villains working for powerful backers (Hammer; Edwin Cord, who blackmails the military). The only route in the collected graph is from Firebrand to Firepower, 7 links: Firebrand → Flying Tiger → Tiger Shark → Attuma → Orka → Iron Monger → Guardsman → Firepower. Firepower is linked only to Iron Monger and Guardsman, so the path has to go around through a group of sea-themed villains (Tiger Shark, Attuma, Orka) before it reaches them.

2. Doom 2099 and Kristoff Vernard: two heirs to Doctor Doom

Doom 2099 is a future version of Victor von Doom who retakes Latveria and becomes its monarch again. Kristoff Vernard is Doom’s adopted son and heir, “formerly known as Kristoff von Doom and Dr. Doom”, who rules Latveria in Doom’s absence. Both pages are, in effect, about someone wearing Doctor Doom’s armour and ruling Latveria. The category does not contain Doctor Doom himself, so the obvious bridge is not in the graph. The only directed path runs from Vernard to Doom 2099, 6 links: Vernard → Nathaniel Richards → Maker → Molecule Man → Super-Adaptoid → Phalanx → Doom 2099. Doom 2099’s page does not lead back to Vernard.

3. Masked Marauder and Gentleman: crime bosses who stage plots against Spider-Man

Masked Marauder is an already successful crime leader “with men, money, and resources at his command”, first noticed in a confrontation with Spider-Man, and who briefly becomes the central villain of Daredevil. He has his men impersonate Daredevil to set the two heroes against each other and later tries to take over the Maggia. The Gentleman is an underworld mastermind “known for conducting elaborate plans to increase his own wealth” who has the Chameleon recruit the Sinister Six for a “Day of Terror” against Spider-Man. The shared pattern is a crime boss who runs an organisation and uses hired villains in elaborate, many-step schemes against Spider-Man. This is the only pair with a finite directed path both ways, 6 links each: from Masked Marauder via Gladiator (Melvin Potter), Mister Fear, Eel, Black Cat and Lizard; from Gentleman via Mysterio, Black Cat, Lizard, Mister Hyde and Gladiator. The two characters come from different periods (the Marauder from 1966 comics, the Gentleman from novels and then a 2016 comic), which fits their separate neighbourhoods.

4. Gorgon and Lady Gorgon: mostly a shared name

Gorgon is Gorgon Petragon of the Inhumans’ Royal Family, with hooves that produce seismic waves and a role as Black Bolt’s bodyguard. Lady Gorgon (Tanya Adrian) is a jōnin of the Hand and an enemy of the Punisher who uses telepathy. After reading both pages we could not find much that they share beyond the name and a general “Marvel character biography” layout, so we read this match as mostly a name effect, like the Firebrand and Firepower alias pages. The graph keeps them far apart: the only directed path runs from Lady Gorgon, 7 links, through Rampage, Wrecker, Hulk, Leader, Super-Skrull and Quicksilver to Gorgon.

5. Condor and Flying Tiger: winged villains from different corners

Condor is a winged criminal from the Bird People and an enemy of Nova; Flying Tiger is a former football player whose costume lets him fly, a mercenary who fights Spider-Woman and Iron Man. Both are minor flyers whose pages are mostly lists of appearances and teams, and both end up on the villain side of Helmut Zemo’s plots: Condor joins the villains attacking S.H.I.E.L.D. after Zemo restores his memory, Flying Tiger joins Zemo’s New Masters. Condor links to Blastaar, Diamondhead, Powerhouse, Red Raven and Sphinx, and Flying Tiger to Eel, Firebrand, Locksmith and Tiger Shark; the two sets do not overlap. The only directed path runs from Flying Tiger, 6 links: Tiger Shark, Egghead, Puppet Master, Sphinx, Diamondhead, Condor.

More candidates from the full table

Three further pairs from the 49, found after the all-pairs run. For these we read only the opening part of each page (not the full text) and checked the cosine and the paths in the graph, so they are candidates for a closer reading, not findings.

  • Jonas Harrow and Whiplash (cosine 0.6359; Harrow→Whiplash 7 links, Whiplash→Harrow 6). Harrow is a disgraced surgeon who, in the words of his creator on the page, is “a Tony Stark for bad guys”: he enhances criminals for a fee (Hammerhead, Kangaroo) and supplies villains such as Living Laser. Whiplash is a gifted electrical technician who becomes a weapons designer and assassin for the Maggia and later works for Justin Hammer. Our reading: both are technically skilled hired helpers to other criminals, in different rogues’ galleries (Spider-Man’s and Iron Man’s). Paths: Harrow → Kangaroo → Grizzly → Jackal → Spider-Girl → Iron Man 2020 → Blizzard → Whiplash; and Whiplash → Scorpio → Norman Osborn → Jackal → Grizzly → Kangaroo → Harrow.
  • Human Cannonball and Thunderball (cosine 0.6460; Thunderball→Human Cannonball 6 links, no path the other way). Both pages describe the character mainly as a member of a villain team: Human Cannonball of the Circus of Crime, Thunderball of the Wrecking Crew. Path: Thunderball → Hulk → Maestro → Spider-Girl → Spider-Bitch → Ringmaster → Human Cannonball.
  • Masked Marauder and Mysterio (cosine 0.6778; 5 links each way). Mysterio’s page says he is a Spider-Man enemy who has also been a major villain of Daredevil; the Masked Marauder briefly served as the central villain of Daredevil and first came to public attention in a clash with Spider-Man. We read only the opening of the long Mysterio page, so treat this as a candidate, not a finding. Paths: Masked Marauder → Gladiator → Mister Fear → Eel → Sin → Mysterio; and Mysterio → Black Cat → Lizard → Mister Hyde → Gladiator → Masked Marauder.

Why would text be close when links are far?

↑

The five pairs suggest a few mechanisms. What the pages say is stated first; the causes are our reading.

  • Shared role, separate storylines. Condor and Flying Tiger are interchangeable in function (a minor flying criminal, later a recruit for a Zemo team), yet one is an enemy of Nova and the other of Spider-Woman and Iron Man. Doom 2099 and Vernard play the same role (ruler of Latveria in Doom’s armour) in different eras. Masked Marauder and the Gentleman are the same kind of schemer in different decades. A link is usually placed where characters meet, and the pages we read do not describe these characters meeting.
  • Shared name or page template. Firebrand/Firepower are both multi-character alias pages, and Gorgon/Lady Gorgon share little more than a name. For these two the embedding is closer to matching the title word and the page layout than to matching the plot.
  • How big are the name, franchise and length effects? These are rough proxies, not tested measures. All of them are computed over the 67,161 pairs that do not involve Naga, Tundra or Shepard, and “the 712” are the pairs among them with a cosine of at least 0.60 (whatever their reachability: 455 reachable both ways, 192 one way, 65 not at all).
    • Shared title word. Two characters share one if their names (without any bracketed part) have a word in common other than “the, of, and, a, Marvel, comics, character, man, woman”, such as “Gorgon” or “Master”. 106 pairs do; their mean cosine is 0.577 against 0.388 for all other pairs. But they are only 40 of the 712, so a shared name is not the main driver of high cosines.
    • Shared franchise. A page’s “franchise” is whichever of 17 hero and team names (Spider-Man, Iron Man, Captain America, Thor, Hulk, Daredevil, Fantastic Four, X-Men, Avengers, Punisher, Wolverine, Doctor Strange, Nova, Spider-Woman, Black Panther, Luke Cage, Namor) its text mentions most, if at least 3 times (303 of the 370 pages have one). Both pages share it in 446 of the 712 pairs (63%), but in only 6.9% of all 67,161 pairs and in 11 of the 49 far-but-reachable pairs (22%). Franchise vocabulary thus helps explain high cosines in general, and the far pairs are mostly cross-franchise.
    • Alias pages. A page counts as an alias page if its first 500 characters say it is the name or alias of several characters, or of two to five (wording such as “the name of several supervillains” or “an alias used by two supervillains”): 82 of the 367 pages. Both pages are alias pages in 83 of the 712 pairs (11.7%) against 4.9% of all pairs; Firebrand–Firepower is one of them.
    • Page length. The Pearson correlation between the cosine and the logarithm of the word count of the shorter page is 0.29, so pairs of longer pages tend to be more similar.
  • Missing hubs. The obvious link between the two Dooms goes through a page that is not in the data. Since only links inside the category count, the distance partly reflects how the dataset was cut, not only Marvel’s story world.
  • Direction. In four of the five pairs a path exists in only one direction. A directed path from A to B does not mean that a reader of B can get back to A, so the same pair can look near from one side and unreachable from the other.

What the data supports: each pair has a high cosine and a shortest directed path of at least 6 links in the collected graph, and the page texts have clear shared elements (except for the Gorgon pair, which is mostly a name effect). What it does not support: that links should exist between these pages. Editors may have good reasons to leave out a link, and a link is not a statement of similarity.

Limitations

↑
  • The model. MiniLM reads at most 256 word-pieces per passage, and a page vector is a plain average of its passages, so long pages become blurred averages. The model is not trained on comics, so names, formatting and template sentences can weigh as much as plot.
  • Cosines are model scores, not measurements of meaning. All 68,265 pairs are compared, but a high cosine only says that two pages use similar words and sentences in the model’s space. Library versions of the first, top-12 run were not recorded; the new run agrees with it to within rounding.
  • The network is the 370 category pages only. Links to heroes, gods, teams and other pages are not in the data, so some “far” pairs may be close in the full Wikipedia graph. Long directed paths are partly a sign of how few links a page has (Firepower has two).
  • We dropped all pairs without a directed path. 24,923 of the 68,265 pairs have no path in either direction, many involving pages with no links at all. Those are the most “structurally far” pairs of all, so this report looks only at pairs that are far but still reachable.
  • Hand-picked examples. The five pairs were chosen from the seven that have a cosine of at least 0.66 (among 49 with at least 0.60), after reading the pages. The explanations are our reading of the text, not tested hypotheses.
  • Page text is a snapshot from the extracts API, and its length varies widely (87 to 18,047 words), which changes how much an embedding can say.

Conclusion

↑

Who is semantically close but structurally far away? Characters who play the same role, or who share a name, but belong to different storylines: the Iron Man armour villains Firebrand and Firepower (7 links), the two heirs of Doctor Doom, Doom 2099 and Kristoff Vernard (6 links), the Spider-Man schemers Masked Marauder and Gentleman (6 links in both directions), the two minor flyers Condor and Flying Tiger (6 links) and, mostly through the name, Gorgon and Lady Gorgon (7 links). The link network records who meets whom, the embedding records who sounds like whom, and these pairs live in the gap. A caveat that matters as much as the examples: some of the distance comes from the data cut (a missing Doctor Doom page, only category-internal links, one-way links), not from the stories alone.
